What TFR is built to do

TFR is an asset backed by OTR, not a separate token competing with it for attention. It exists to aggregate OTR's trading volume, open up new arbitrage opportunities across pools, and give OTR holders more ways to earn and compound than holding alone offers.

✓

Fair launch, no exceptions

There was no airdrop, no presale, and no insider allocation or advance information given to anyone before launch. Every wallet gets access to TFR the same way, at the same time: by swapping OTR for it on the open market. Nobody, including the team, got a head start.

⇆

Aggregates OTR's volume

TFR is only ever obtainable by swapping OTR for it. There's no direct PLS or stablecoin route in. Every buy, every arbitrage trade, and every staking claim that gets converted into TFR routes real volume through OTR first. TFR doesn't pull attention away from OTR; it funnels activity back into it.

≡

Creates arbitrage opportunities

TFR's dollar price is mechanically tied to OTR's price times the TFR/OTR pool ratio, so the two can drift out of sync with each other and with the wider market. That gap is exactly what arbitrage traders look for, and closing it means more volume moving through both OTR and TFR, not less.

∞

More APR and compounding for holders

Two staking pools exist so far: stake OTR to earn TFR, and stake TFR to earn TFR, letting holders compound in more than one direction instead of just holding. Both are funded from a dedicated reserve, not fresh token printing, and neither has any admin function once deployed.

🔒

Permanently locked liquidity

The TFR/OTR pool itself is burned to the dead address the moment it's created. There is no unlock date, ever. Every OTR spent buying TFR deepens a pool that nobody, including the deployer, can ever withdraw from.

Tested, not just theorized

The "aggregates volume, creates arbitrage, accelerates faster than OTR" claims above aren't projections; they were measured against real historical PulseChain mainnet state, replayed on a local Hardhat fork (not a synthetic simulation).

How to read this: $50 is spent in each row, on the token in the "Scenario" column. The OTR and TFR columns show how much each token's own price moved as a result of that same $50 trade: same trade, same moment, both columns, every row.

Scenario ($50 spent)OTRTFR
Buying TFR directly with OTR No price change Up 15.03%
Buying TFR from PLS Up 2.68% Up 17.85%

Why OTR doesn't move in the first row: this trade goes straight from OTR to TFR. It never touches the OTR/WPLS pool where OTR's own market price is actually set. TFR still moves because the trade directly shifts the TFR/OTR pool's ratio, which is the only thing TFR's price is made of.

Why TFR moves more than OTR in the second row: TFR's price is always OTR's price multiplied by the TFR/OTR pool ratio, so whenever OTR goes up, TFR automatically goes up the same percentage with it, for free, before anything else happens. This trade adds a second effect on top: routing all the way through to TFR also shifts the TFR/OTR ratio directly, the same way row one did. The two effects stack, which is why TFR's 17.85% move is bigger than OTR's 2.68%, not just equal to it.

Every one of TFR's price moves in these tests traces back to real OTR-side activity. TFR isn't competing with OTR for volume; the numbers show it amplifying it. As with any past testing, this describes how the mechanism behaved under the scenarios tested, not a guarantee of future results.

Why this matters for OTR holders

None of this requires OTR holders to do anything differently; it works in the background off routine trading activity. But for anyone who wants to participate directly, TFR adds a second, permanently-locked venue for OTR's liquidity, a price mechanism that mirrors and amplifies OTR's own moves (see the Tokenomics tab for the exact math), and staking options that didn't exist before it.
